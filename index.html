About

Born and raised in New England, USA, I moved to Texas State University at 17 years old to study biology. From there I finished college at 21 and moved to California to study theology, Greek, church history and community service for two years. My journey at 23 years old brought me to Brazil to the University of Sao Paulo where I earned a Master's degree and PhD in Cell Biology. My post-doctoral work (carried out during the COVID pandemic) was focused on the molecular epidemiology of papillomavirus variants in men. Most actively starting in 2016 I built two parallel careers that reinforce each other. As an independent innovation strategist, I advise biotech, medtech, pharma, and neurotech organizations on commercialization, market readiness, go/no-go decisions, and business development — engagements built entirely through referral. As a scientific writer and researcher, I have co-authored twelve peer-reviewed publications and supported over 120 researchers and academics with manuscript development, editing, and translation.

My most recent embedded roles — Head of Strategy & Innovation at a neurotech company and Head of Commercial Development at a Swiss biotech firm expanding into Barcelona — which sat within my continuous independent practice rather than as separate employment, giving me hands-on strategic leadership without stepping away from my broader client base. I hold dual US-EU citizenship, am fluent in Portuguese and English with conversational Spanish, and I bring cross-cultural fluency from years of work across Brazil, Portugal, Spain, the Philippines, the US, and Switzerland.

Teaching & community

Innovation outside the lab

LEGO bricks and biology education

My undergraduate honors thesis, developed with MIT's Dr. Kathy Vandiver, explored using LEGO bricks to teach protein structure and folding. As a teaching assistant, I extended the method using bricks, clay, and balloons to help physical therapy students model tissue types — an approach that proved especially valuable for a vision-impaired student in the course.

I also built a LEGO model of the citric acid cycle, purely as a creative exercise between experiments.
